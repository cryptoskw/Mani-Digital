Internal linking SEO guide

Build internal links that explain the whole website.

A practical internal-linking framework for connecting service pages, location pages, industries, case studies and guides into a crawlable commercial structure.

Browse all SEO guides
01 / THE OUTCOME

Internal links should help customers move to the next useful answer while showing search engines which pages are related, specific and strategically important.

Design clear hub-and-cluster relationships

Improve discovery of priority pages

Use descriptive anchors without over-optimisation

Find and repair orphan or buried content

02 / THE CONTEXT

A link is both navigation and editorial context

The main menu cannot carry every relationship on a growing website. Contextual links inside useful copy explain why one page supports another and move authority toward the pages closest to business value.

A strong structure normally flows from broad hubs to focused services and guides, then returns through breadcrumbs, related resources and relevant calls to action. The result should feel natural to a visitor even if SEO did not exist.

Best suited to

Websites with growing content libraries

Service businesses with several locations

Sites containing orphan pages

Teams restructuring navigation

03 / THE WORK

Seven rules for a useful internal-link system

Apply these rules to templates and editorial reviews so the structure improves whenever a new page is published.

01

Choose the commercial owners

Identify the pages that own the main services and markets. Supporting content should reinforce those URLs rather than compete with them.

02

Create meaningful hubs

Use service, industry, area and resource hubs to organise related pages and explain the distinctions between them.

03

Link where the question arises

Place contextual links inside the paragraph where the related service or guide becomes the visitor's logical next step.

04

Write descriptive anchors

Use wording that tells the reader what is behind the link. Vary phrasing naturally and avoid forcing the exact same keyword everywhere.

05

Control crawl depth

Keep priority pages within a few useful clicks of the homepage or a strong hub. Deep nesting can hide content even when it appears in the sitemap.

06

Repair broken and orphaned pages

Crawl the site after each structural release. Redirect retired URLs and ensure every indexable page receives at least one deliberate link.

07

Review link concentration

Template links can overwhelm contextual signals. Confirm that important pages receive relevant editorial links, not only repeated footer navigation.

05 / STRAIGHT ANSWERS

Questions this guide should settle.

Reviewed 15 September 2026

How many internal links should a page have?

There is no fixed number. Include the links that genuinely help the visitor continue the task and make the topic hierarchy clear.

Do footer links help SEO?

They support discovery and navigation, but contextual links usually communicate a more specific relationship. Use both according to purpose.

Should I use exact-match anchor text?

Descriptive anchors are useful, but repeating the same exact phrase unnaturally is unnecessary. Write for clarity and vary the wording where appropriate.

What is an orphan page?

An orphan page has no crawlable internal link pointing to it. A sitemap may expose it to search engines, but users and site authority have no normal route to reach it.
