On-page SEO checklist

Optimise the page without destroying the message.

A complete on-page SEO checklist for service pages and guides, from search intent and headings to proof, media, internal links and conversion.

Browse all SEO guides
01 / THE OUTCOME

On-page SEO should make a page clearer and more useful. If optimisation turns the copy into repeated keywords, it has moved away from the customer and the search task.

Match one page to one clear search task

Strengthen titles, headings and copy naturally

Add proof and useful supporting detail

Connect the page to the wider topic cluster

02 / THE CONTEXT

The page must satisfy a person before it can create business value

A title can earn a click, but the page must quickly confirm relevance. The visitor needs to recognise the service or question, understand the answer, see credible evidence and know what to do next.

Search engines also need consistent signals across the URL, title, headings, body, media, links and structured data. Those signals should describe the same page purpose rather than chase unrelated phrases.

Best suited to

Service-page writers

Small-business website owners

Marketing teams reviewing content

Developers building reusable templates

03 / THE WORK

Twelve checks before a page is ready to publish

Treat the checks as one editorial review rather than isolated ranking tricks.

01

Intent and page purpose

State the exact search task, audience and action this URL owns. Confirm that another page is not already doing the same job.

02

Title and description

Write a specific title that reflects the main intent and brand. Use the description to clarify value and earn the click without making unsupported claims.

03

Heading structure

Use one descriptive H1 and logical H2 or H3 sections that help readers scan the argument. Headings should not be a list of keyword variations.

04

Opening answer

Confirm relevance early. Explain what the service or guide provides, who it helps and what the reader will understand or achieve.

05

Depth and evidence

Include process, limitations, examples, decisions, subject expertise and proof that competing generic pages cannot easily reproduce.

06

Media and accessibility

Use images or diagrams where they improve understanding, reserve dimensions, compress files and write alternative text for meaning rather than keywords.

07

Internal and external links

Link to the parent service, useful supporting pages and authoritative sources where claims require verification. Keep anchor wording descriptive.

08

Conversion and trust

Provide a clear next step, contact expectations, business identity, policy information and enough qualification to reduce poor-fit enquiries.

05 / STRAIGHT ANSWERS

Questions this guide should settle.

Reviewed 15 September 2026

Where should the main keyword appear?

Use it naturally in the title, H1, opening context and relevant copy when that language is accurate. Do not force an exact phrase into every heading.

How long should an SEO page be?

Long enough to complete the search task and support the business decision. A focused page may outperform a longer one filled with repetition.

Should every page include FAQ schema?

Only add FAQs that are genuinely visible and useful. Structured data should accurately describe the page; it does not guarantee a special search result.

When should a page be updated?

Update when the offer, facts, search intent, competition or user questions change, or when performance evidence reveals a clear weakness.
